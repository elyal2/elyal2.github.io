## 1. Context and Challenge

A real estate organization seeks to modernize its software development practices through artificial intelligence, evolving from code assistants towards agentic collaboration models and more autonomous systems. The challenge is to improve productivity without losing control over quality, security, traceability, or the knowledge accumulated across projects. The absence of executable specifications, shared rules, persistent memory, and validation mechanisms can lead to inconsistent results, context debt, and an expanded risk surface in environments where agents are connected to corporate tools.

## 2. Technical Solution

The proposed approach is based on Spec-Driven Development, in which requirements are formalized before code is generated and validated through structured planning, execution, and review cycles. The solution incorporates specialized agents for architecture, planning, code review, documentation validation, and runbook generation, together with reusable microagents and domain-specific skills. The architecture combines versioned specifications, project rules, integration and quality pipelines, persistent semantic memory through Retrieval-Augmented Generation, and controlled access to tools through the Model Context Protocol. The BDD approach converts business scenarios into verifiable criteria and enables unit, integration, end-to-end, acceptance, and regression testing. Governance is supported by least-privilege permissions, subagent isolation, human review at control points, decision traceability, sensitive-information classification, and declarative metadata management.

## 3. Business Impact and Results

As this is a methodological and training proposal, the results should be considered expected and remain subject to the execution of a pilot and its subsequent scale-up. The approach is expected to accelerate the preparation of specifications, work plans, reviews, and documentation, reducing manual tasks and improving consistency across teams. Specialized agents and quality gates would make it possible to scale change reviews and test generation across more projects while maintaining consistent controls. Persistent memory and versioned assets would support continuity between sessions and the reuse of knowledge. Finally, structured rules, skills, and workflows would provide the internal team with greater autonomy, supported by a gradual model of supervised automation and sufficient traceability for regulated environments.
