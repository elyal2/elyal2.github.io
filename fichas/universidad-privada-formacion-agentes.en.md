## 1. Context and Challenge

A private university sought to develop its team’s capabilities to create intelligent agents that could converse with students and staff while also executing actions across internal processes. The challenge was to combine natural language understanding, task automation and sound security practices to address real use cases in admissions, academic administration and teaching quality. The organisation needed to reduce its dependence on external support, accelerate automation adoption and establish a reusable methodology for extending these use cases to other departments.

## 2. Technical Solution

A practical training and co-creation programme was designed using Copilot Studio as the conversational interface and Power Automate as the action execution engine. The functional architecture supports agents that capture information, classify requests, query and update records, send notifications and orchestrate processes through integrations across the Microsoft ecosystem. The programme combines foundational concepts, guided exercises and autonomous use-case development, resulting in two pilots: academic request management and the analysis and classification of feedback for teaching quality. The solution includes flows with triggers, actions, conditions, branches, error handling, traceability and validation in a controlled environment. It also incorporates recommendations on governance, permissions, authorised connectors, DLP policies, secure instruction design, and functional and technical documentation to facilitate ongoing operation and future development.

## 3. Business Impact and Results

As this is a proposed training initiative that has not yet been delivered, the results described are expected outcomes. The initiative will enable internal teams to design, maintain and scale agents and automations autonomously, without continuous reliance on external support. It is expected to improve response times and reduce manual work in request management while increasing end-to-end traceability. For teaching quality, the solution is expected to centralise information, automate the classification and prioritisation of feedback, and support the early detection of sensitive situations. The pilots, templates and evolution backlog will provide a reusable foundation for extending the solution to new areas and accelerating adoption.
