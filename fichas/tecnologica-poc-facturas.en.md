## 1. Context and Challenge

A foundation focused on technological research and social impact wanted to assess the feasibility of automating an existing supplier-document analysis process. The challenge involved processing invoices, delivery notes, contracts, data processing agreements and other documents related to contractual relationships, while reducing manual intervention without replacing the responsibilities of finance or accounting teams. The initiative was structured as a limited proof of concept, restricted to the use cases defined by the organisation and excluding customisations or additional integrations beyond the agreed scope.

## 2. Technical Solution

The proposed solution consists of configuring and adapting a general-purpose artificial intelligence system on the technology partner’s infrastructure, using a controlled environment to analyse supplier documentation. The solution will rely on previously anonymised documents, evaluation criteria defined by the organisation and suitable data structures for the selected use cases. The design includes confidentiality controls, data minimisation, deletion of documents and generated data at the end of the proof of concept, as well as human oversight, traceability, system documentation, continuous risk management and data-quality validation. The organisation will be responsible for providing the required infrastructure, ensuring that no personal data is included and validating the applicable regulatory requirements.

## 3. Business Impact and Results

As this is a proposed proof of concept that has not yet been executed, the expected outcome is to validate the technical and operational feasibility of automating the analysis of supplier invoices and delivery notes. If feasibility is confirmed, the system could reduce the manual effort associated with document review, accelerate the identification and extraction of relevant information and provide a foundation for subsequently scaling the process to other authorised documents or use cases. The initiative is also expected to support a controlled assessment of the solution’s accuracy, transparency, security and regulatory suitability, while maintaining human oversight and without replacing the responsibilities of finance or accounting teams.
